# Designing an unbiased price survey for fab materials

> **SIMULATED example.** The responses in `simulated_responses.csv` are generated by
> `survey/process_survey.py` (fixed seed) to show the processing steps. They are not real
> answers and not TechInsights data.

Public trade data gives a **unit value** (value / kg, all grades mixed, two months late). A survey
gives a **transaction price** for a precisely defined product, faster. The two are complementary:
the survey is the precise but small source, trade data the broad but blurry one.

## 1. Target population and frame
| Element | Choice for the example (anhydrous hydrofluoric acid, EU, Q3-2026) |
|---|---|
| Population | Companies that **bought or sold** AHF delivered in the EU during the quarter |
| Frame | Buyers: fabs, wet-chemical blenders, etch-chemical formulators. Sellers: producers and distributors. Built from public registers (REACH registrants, industry association member lists) and customer lists |
| Strata | Role (buyer / seller) x size (annual volume < 500 t, 500-5,000 t, > 5,000 t) |
| Unit | One respondent per company and stratum (no double counting of group subsidiaries) |

Asking **both sides** of the market is the first defence against bias: buyers tend to quote low,
sellers high, and both are kept separate until weighting.

## 2. Sampling
- Stratified random sample, with **all large companies** included (take-all stratum) because they
  carry most of the volume.
- Target 30-50 completed responses per quarter; minimum 5 per published cell, otherwise the cell
  is merged or suppressed (confidentiality and reliability).
- Same panel each quarter where possible (price *changes* are then measured on matched
  respondents, which removes composition noise).

## 3. Questionnaire rules (see `questionnaire.md`)
- **No anchoring**: no example price, no pre-filled value, no price ranges to tick, no reference to
  last quarter's published figure.
- **Neutral wording**: "What price did you pay" - never "how much did prices rise".
- **Precise definitions** printed next to the question: purity, concentration, volume band,
  delivery terms (Incoterm), currency, period, whether the price is contract or spot.
- **Actual transactions** only: invoiced price for deliveries in the period, not list prices or
  expectations.
- "Don't know / no transaction" is always an option, so nobody is pushed to guess.
- Order of materials randomised between respondents; one material per screen.
- Confidentiality statement: only aggregates of at least 5 respondents are published.

## 4. Processing (implemented in `process_survey.py`)
1. **Standardise**: convert every answer to EUR per kg, CIF/DAP EU basis
   (USD with the ECB monthly rate; per tonne to per kg; FOB answers + a documented freight
   allowance).
2. **Validate**: reject answers outside the plausible range of the data dictionary, then flag
   robust outliers (more than 3.5 modified z-scores from the median, median / MAD). Outliers are
   **checked with the respondent** before any exclusion; in the simulation they are excluded.
3. **Weight**: design weight = stratum population / respondents in the stratum, times the
   reported volume (capped at the 90th percentile so one giant buyer cannot drive the result).
4. **Estimate**: volume-weighted mean and weighted median, with a bootstrap 90% interval.
5. **Consolidate with public data**: compare with the Comext unit value for the same quarter. A
   gap inside the expected band (grade premium) confirms both sources; a gap outside it opens a
   discrepancy card, exactly as for two public sources.

## 5. Bias checklist
| Bias | Counter-measure |
|---|---|
| Anchoring | No numbers in the questions |
| Social desirability / strategic answers | Both sides of the market; matched-panel changes; confidentiality |
| Non-response | Reminders; response rate by stratum reported; weights recomputed on respondents |
| Coverage | Frame refreshed each year; new entrants added |
| Definition drift | Product specification printed on each question; specification changes = new series |
| Outliers / unit errors | Standardisation and robust outlier test, checked with the respondent |
